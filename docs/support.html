BibleText — Support

Thanks for using BibleText, a clean and fast way to read and study the Bible. Here's how to get help and answers to common questions.

Getting around

Tap Read to read, Books to pick a book, and Search to search by keyword. Use Go to at the top to jump straight to a reference like John 3:16. Your place is remembered automatically, and the app works offline once the text has loaded. Light and dark themes follow your device.

Red words, poetry, and text size

On iPhone, iPad, Android, and Mac the words of Jesus are shown in red by default — turn that off under the gear (top right) → Reading, where you can also change the scripture text size. Poetic passages — the Psalms, Proverbs, Job, and much of the prophets — are set as poetry: each authored line begins a new line, the way it appears in print. Text you copy or share keeps those lines.

Switching translations

Tap the translation name under the title to switch between the World English Bible, the World English Bible (Catholic) — with the deuterocanonical books — and the Berean Standard Bible (all public domain), and the New King James Version.

The NKJV is served through API.Bible and works as soon as you install the app. You can also use your own free API.Bible key instead — create one, add the NKJV to it, and paste it into Settings → Translations; "Clear" removes it again. The picker still lists the NRSV and LSB greyed out: those are copyrighted translations under evaluation, and they become selectable only if and when licensing is completed.

Sharing a verse — with a note

Select a passage, tap Share, and choose how it goes: a typeset image card, text with a citation, or a link that opens the same verses for your friend. You can attach a short note of your own to a link — it travels inside the link itself, through whatever messaging app you send it with, and opens with your message beside the passage in the translation you were reading. No account is involved, and nothing passes through any server of ours. If your friend doesn't have the app, the link shows the passage — and the note — right in their browser.

Notes you've received and sent are collected under Notes on the Search tab, searchable and sorted. A passage can carry more than one note — the note says so ("1 of 3 on this passage") and tapping the count steps through them. You can delete all stored notes from the gear → notes settings.

Listening to a chapter

Tap the speaker in the reading header to play the chapter: a public-domain recorded narration where one exists, or (on phones, tablets, and Mac) your device reading it aloud. Playback continues into the next chapter, and on phones and tablets it keeps going from the lock screen.

AI study features (optional)

The AI features (Find, Explain, and context/translation analysis) are optional and use your own AI provider key. To turn them on, tap the gear (top right) → choose a provider (Google Gemini, OpenAI, Anthropic, or SpaceXAI/Grok) → paste your API key → Test key. Your key is stored in the system Keychain on iPhone and iPad, and on Mac, Windows, Linux, and Android in the app's own settings on that device. When you use an AI feature, your Find search or the passage and study action you selected are sent directly to the provider you chose, using your key. See the Privacy Policy.

The text doesn't seem complete

On first launch BibleText shows the Gospels immediately and downloads the rest of the Bible in the background; a banner indicates this. If it stalls, make sure you have a connection and reopen the app — the download resumes automatically.
