VERSION 1.2.4 (macOS)

BibleText is a Bible reader. No account, login, or registration is required, and reading, search, navigation, notes, and cross-references need no AI-provider key. Downloaded scripture stays readable and searchable offline; downloads and optional provider services need connectivity when used.

This is the first Mac App Store release, built from the same 1.2.4 codebase as the iOS app on this record; these notes cover what is specific to the Mac.

STUDY GESTURE ON THE MAC
Selection actions live on the right-click menu rather than a touch popover. Select any passage and right-click: the menu offers the system's Copy and Look Up, Cross-references, a Share submenu (with citation, as image, as link, with note), and — only when an AI provider is configured — a Study with AI submenu (Explain, Analyze context, Analyze translation). Settings → Assistant defaults to None, so no AI items appear and no AI setup is needed for review.

APP SANDBOX AND THE ONE-TIME MIGRATION
The Store edition runs in the App Sandbox. On the first launch that creates its container, macOS moves an existing directly downloaded install's preferences (notes, settings, reading position) into it, via the bundled container-migration manifest. That is a local file move by macOS; nothing is transmitted. With no prior install there is nothing to move and the app starts fresh, so the migration is not visible during review.

KEYS AND TRANSLATIONS
The App Store binary intentionally contains an obfuscated, link-time-injected API.Bible fallback used only to fetch the licensed New King James Version; a reader can replace it with their own key. The NKJV text is fetched from API.Bible, not packaged in the app or published on the website.

No AI-provider key is bundled. The optional Study with AI features stay off until the reader enters their own provider key in Settings → Assistant. That key is stored on the device and requests go directly to the selected provider.

The full WEB, WEB Catholic, and BSB texts are fetched and cached at runtime. The only embedded scripture is a small WEB Gospels seed shown on first launch; the Gospel-parallels dataset holds references, not another translation.

SHARED NOTES — HOW TO TEST
Shared notes are the one place the app displays text written by another person.

To send: select a verse, right-click, choose Share with note, type a short message, and pass on the ordinary https://bibletext.co.uk link.

To receive on the Mac: paste the link into Notes or Messages and click it there — with BibleText installed it opens in the app, passage and message side by side (the app claims the domain via Universal Links). A URL typed into the browser address bar opens the website instead, per standard Universal Links behaviour; pasting it into the app's search box also works.

PRIVACY: the message is encoded in the URL fragment and travels only through the channel the sender chose. There is no server, account, user directory, or developer copy, and the fragment is not sent to the website host. A received message is rendered as plain text with neutral attribution, never as markup or in the app's voice. The recipient can delete one note, delete all in Settings, or disable notes entirely.

WHAT TO EXERCISE
Select a verse and right-click to see the selection menu described above. The audio button narrates the World English Bible and Berean Standard Bible with human recordings; the NKJV and deuterocanonical books use on-device speech. With Words of Jesus enabled (Settings → Reading) the spoken words in any Gospel passage are red, following each translation's own publisher markings.

Three 1.2.4 fixes are visible here: cross-references now resolve on the closing doxology of Romans (16:25-27), where they were absent in every translation; a shared note no longer reserves a screen-height empty band around its passage; and a translator's footnote anchored in an omitted verse — Matthew 17:21, 18:11, or 23:14 — is kept rather than discarded.
